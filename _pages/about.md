I am an Assistant Professor and Fellow of the Wilbur J. Cohen Professorship in Health and Social Policy at the [University of Texas at Austin’s Lyndon B. Johnson School of Public Affairs](https://lbj.utexas.edu/) and the Department of Government (by courtesy). I am also a faculty associate of the [Lozano Long Institute of Latin American Studies (LLILAS)](https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/llilas/) at UT-Austin, and an affiliated researcher of the [Fundação Getulio Vargas](https://portal.fgv.br/). I received my PhD in Political Science from the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://www.unc.edu/).

My research focuses on the Americas, with a particular emphasis on how violence, crime, and state security institutions shape politics. My book project, *Uniformed Representation: The Electoral Rise of Security Officials in Democratic Brazil*, investigates the rise of law enforcement and military officials as electoral candidates, the conditions that facilitate their political entry, and the implications of their growing political influence. This research received the [APSA’s Best Dissertation in Human Rights Award](https://apsanet.org/membership/organized-sections/organized-section-awards/past-awards/section-36/#dissertation), SPSA’s Best Graduate Student Paper Award, and an honorable mention for the QMMR Qualitative Evidence Award. I am also interested in social policy (and how it relates to topics of violence and crime), public health, and the political representation of certain occupations (e.g., police, doctors, experts) more broadly. 

My research has been published or is forthcoming in journals such as the *British Journal of Political Science*, *Comparative Political Studies*, *Political Research Quarterly*, *Research & Politics*, the *Journal of Criminal Justice*, and the *Journal of Politics in Latin America* and has been referenced by news outlets such as *O Estado de São Paulo*, *A Folha de São Paulo*, and *El Diaro de Júarez*. I have written popular press pieces for The *Washington Post* and *Lawfare*. My work has been supported by the H.F. Guggenheim Foundation, Fulbright-Hays, the U.S. Department of State, and USAID, among other organizations.
